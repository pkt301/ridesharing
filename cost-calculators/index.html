How to use these calculators to get leads for your own budget

Most people only remember today's petrol price. Calculators make the full picture visible: daily fuel, monthly burn, parking, maintenance, and yearly totals. Once you see the yearly number, shared travel becomes an obvious experiment.

Recommended workflow

  1. Enter your real one-way distance and commuting days.
  2. Add parking and a realistic maintenance estimate.
  3. Note the yearly total.
  4. Change “shared seats” to 2, 3, or 4 and compare fuel savings.
  5. Use the fuel-price impact tool when rates rise by ₹5–₹10/litre.

Frequently asked questions about travel costs

Should I include parking in commute cost?

Yes. Office parking and paid lots often add thousands per year and are easy to forget when people only track fuel.

Is share fuel cost a taxi fare?

No. On Rydo it is a voluntary fuel/toll contribution between private travellers on a similar route — not commercial transport pricing.

How many seats should I assume?

Start with your realistic average occupancy (often 2–3 for office corridors). Recalculate after two weeks of real matching.

Can students use these calculators?

Absolutely. College routes with stable timings are excellent candidates for shared travel savings.

How to use these commute cost calculators

Indian commuters often underestimate true monthly travel spend because fuel, parking, tolls, and maintenance arrive in separate moments. These calculators help you combine them into one picture — then compare against recurring seat share on Rydo.

Suggested workflow

  1. Estimate daily/yearly solo cost with your real corridor distance and fuel price.
  2. Stress-test with the fuel price impact tool when petrol/diesel moves.
  3. Model per-seat share for a typical car occupancy.
  4. Convert 3–5 weekdays to shared seats and re-check next month.

Are these official government numbers?

No — they are planning estimates based on the inputs you enter.

Does Rydo set the fuel share amount?

No. Participants agree voluntary contributions. Rydo is not a taxi and does not use surge fares.

Why do parking and tolls matter?

On many office belts they rival fuel. Ignoring them understates solo cost and undervalues seat share.
