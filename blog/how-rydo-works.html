How Rydo works

Step-by-step: how Rydo matches riders and captains on the same route, verifies OTP, tracks GPS, and completes trips with fuel share.

Rydo is a route-based carpool and seat share app for private travellers in India. It connects people going the same way so they can share seats and voluntary fuel/toll costs.

For riders

  1. Download from Google Play and sign up.
  2. Open Rider Dashboard, set destination / ride type.
  3. Browse available captains on your route and request.
  4. Share Ride OTP at pickup; track live; complete the trip.

For captains

  1. Go Active on Captain Dashboard with your route.
  2. Accept or reject ride requests.
  3. Verify OTP to start; use Track Ride and messaging.
  4. Complete the trip; review passenger details and history.
Rydo is not a taxi. Suggested amounts are share fuel cost only.

Why this matters for daily travellers in India

Fuel prices, traffic delays, and parking fees quietly drain monthly budgets. Professionals, students, and captains who travel the same corridor every day can turn empty seats into shared savings. The goal is simple: more people per car on routes that already exist, with clear communication and safety tools.

Whether you drive or ride, the habit that compounds is consistency — the same pickup window, the same landmarks, and trusted partners saved as favourites. Over a quarter, that routine often matters more than a one-off discount.

Practical checklist you can use today

  1. Write down your home landmark and office/college gate.
  2. Note your realistic departure window (15 minutes, not one exact minute).
  3. Estimate solo monthly cost with our travel cost calculator.
  4. Match one shared ride this week on the same route.
  5. After a good trip, favourite the partner and repeat.

How Rydo supports this journey

Rydo is built for carpool and seat share. Captains activate routes they already take. Riders request seats on matching corridors. Both sides use OTP verification, in-app messaging, live tracking, and optional trip summaries. Suggested amounts are voluntary fuel and toll contributions — not commercial taxi fares.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Matching at the last second during peak traffic
  • Unclear luggage or seat expectations
  • Skipping OTP because you are “in a hurry”
  • Arguing about money mid-trip instead of agreeing earlier
  • Silent cancellations that strand the other person
